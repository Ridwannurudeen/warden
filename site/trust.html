Evidence / Trust architecture

Four layers. Four different proof boundaries.

Warden gates a proposed action at the caller, leaves a signed decision record, publishes chain continuity, and adds dated audit or marketplace context without collapsing those facts into a universal trust score.

The truth stack

Inspect each layer on its own terms.

Every layer identifies what it proves, what it does not prove, who can verify it, and where the underlying material can be inspected.

01 / Caller-side enforcement

Local enforcement

What it proves
An observed caller trace can show Warden returned a verdict before that proposed action branch.
What it does not prove
That every other execution path is gated.
Who verifies it
The integrating caller through its own control flow.
Where to inspect
Integration contract
safe = WardenClient(local=True, fail_open=False).guard(untrusted_text)

02 / Signed APA evidence

Agent Protection Attestation

What it proves
A valid, fresh issuer signature over the stated endpoint, guard state, and rolling count or explicit unavailable state.
What it does not prove
It does not prove that every request traversed the guard or that a valid key was never stolen.
Who verifies it
Anyone with browser-native WebCrypto and the issuer keys.
Where to inspect
Local attestation verifier

03 / Public transparency

Hash-chained evidence history

What it proves
Internal chain continuity against the signed checkpoint.
What it does not prove
Completeness or an external timestamp when no independent checkpoint has been published.
Who verifies it
Any browser or client that recomputes the canonical chain.
Where to inspect
Transparency Log

Evidence boundary

Signature validity is not endpoint safety.

A signature can establish issuer identity and record integrity. A freshness check can establish whether the signed claim is current under its contract. Neither conclusion independently establishes that an endpoint is uncompromised, universally safe, or correctly gated in every execution path.
